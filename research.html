Research Overview

My research exists at the intersection of geometry, topology, and computation. I view computation not merely as a tool for approximation, but as a rigorous mathematical lens. This relationship works both ways: geometric and topological invariants are critical to maintain in modern algorithms, while computation is increasingly required to develop and prove deep mathematical conjectures. Throughout my career, my guiding philosophy has been the use of intrinsic, coordinate-free mathematical thinking. I focus heavily on exterior calculus and a coordinate-free view of differential geometry to create structure-preserving discretizations of continuous mathematics. By translating continuous differential geometry and exterior calculus into finite-dimensional combinatorial equivalents on simplicial complexes, we build computational methods that exactly preserve topological and geometric invariants. This geometric perspective provides a powerful, unified framework for solving complex problems across mathematical physics, engineering, and modern artificial intelligence.

Foundations: Discrete Exterior Calculus & Differential Geometry

The core foundation of my work is Discrete Exterior Calculus (DEC), a field I originated in my doctoral thesis at Caltech. My work in this area focuses on creating a combinatorial differential geometry using discrete vector bundles with connection on simplicial complexes. Currently, my active projects in this domain involve proving the convergence and stability of DEC for the Hodge-Laplace problem, developing vector bundle-valued DEC, and addressing the discretization of nonlinear terms arising from the wedge product of forms. We are also actively working on the discretization of various tensor types, the rigorous treatment of boundary conditions via boundary complexes, discrete Poincaré operators, and A-infinity structures on simplicial complexes.

Active Applications: Physics, Topology, & Numerical Analysis

We actively apply these structure-preserving methods to the numerical solution of partial differential equations and computational physics. By utilizing DEC and finite element exterior calculus, we analyze well-centered meshing and design conservative integrators for piecewise smooth dynamics. In computational physics, this allows for highly accurate, invariant-preserving simulations of the Navier-Stokes equations for fluids on simplicial surfaces, two-phase flows, compressible flows, elasticity, and flow in porous media. Additionally, in computational topology, we apply these tools to compute harmonic forms on hyperbolic 3-manifolds and embedded surfaces using harmonic series expansions and finite element exterior calculus.

Emerging Frontiers: Geometric Scientific Machine Learning

As researchers in AI increasingly recognize the necessity of structure preservation, we have begun applying our geometric frameworks to machine learning. Our current work involves high-dimensional embeddings of scalar values in tabular data machine learning. Supported by a recent NSF grant starting in June 2026, my group is explicitly combining machine learning with structure-preserving discretizations for PDEs to build robust, geometrically accurate AI models for the physical sciences.

Previous Applications & Historical Work

Because coordinate-free geometry is a universal language, I have previously applied these principles to a diverse range of disciplines. My academic background is rooted in Computer Science, with an early focus on logic and automated theorem proving at Stanford University, which later evolved into research on the structure of satisfiable and unsatisfiable propositional logic sentences using graph theory. Early in my career, I applied a geometric perspective to Computer Graphics as a software engineer at Sun Microsystems. Furthermore, I have worked in astrodynamics, utilizing structure-preserving numerical methods as part of the trajectory design team for the Jupiter Icy Moons Orbiter at the NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory.
