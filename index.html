I am a Professor in the Department of Mathematics at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. I am interested in the interplay between geometry / topology and computation. This relationship works both ways: geometric and topological invariants are critical to maintain in modern computational physics and engineering applications, while computation is increasingly helping to develop and disprove mathematical conjectures.

My current research is: (1) structure-preserving discretization of exterior calculus and differential geometry; (2) application of these in computational physics and engineering. These topics also make contact with algebraic topology and homological algebra. To us, discretization means creating a combinatorial or finite-dimensional version of calculus and differential geometry on simplicial complexes that is suitable for computers. The discrete structures we study and their limitations are mathematically fascinating in their own right, while also being highly useful in applications.

My main applications so far have been to the numerical solution of partial differential equations and the analysis of the methods. In the past, I have also applied these ideas to computer graphics. More recently, researchers in machine learning and AI have started to adopt the structure-preservation philosophy. Indeed, my current NSF grant (started June 2026) explicitly combines machine learning with structure-preservation for PDEs, as detailed in the funding section below. For more information on active and past projects, please see my research page.
